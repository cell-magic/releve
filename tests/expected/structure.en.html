Relevé · http://localhost:8765/structure.html

Automated observation of one rendered page, by rule. It is not a conformity audit: a rule decides what a rule can, and puts the rest to a person.

Method

A browser opened each page as a reader's would, at 1280 px and again at 320 px, and recorded what it showed: the names assistive technology is given, the colours as rendered, the bytes that travelled. 44 criteria then read that record. Each is a rule in plain code; no trained model takes part, so the same record gives the same report.

Changes to make (8)

The main content is marked

A marked main region lets a reader go straight to the content, past what every page repeats. RGAA 9.2 · 12.6 · WCAG 2.4.1 · light work

  • 2 main landmarks are visible
    Change: keep one <main>; give the others the hidden attribute

No element is used as a gap

An element that holds nothing is still announced, or counted, by assistive technology: spacing belongs to the style sheet. RGAA 8.9 · WCAG 1.3.1 · light work

  • 2 empty paragraphs
    Change: remove them; spacing belongs to the style sheet
  • 1 empty heading
    Change: remove it; spacing belongs to the style sheet

Appearance is left to the style sheet

Appearance written into the markup cannot be adapted by a reader's own styles; left to the style sheet, it can. RGAA 10.1 · WCAG 1.3.1 · some work

  • attribute bgcolor used 1 time
    Change: move it to the style sheet
  • <center> used 1 time
    Change: move it to the style sheet
  • <font> used 1 time
    Change: move it to the style sheet
  • attribute color used 1 time
    Change: move it to the style sheet

No window opens by itself

A window that opens by itself moves the reader somewhere they did not choose, and may not be noticed at all. RGAA 13.2 · WCAG 3.2.1 · some work

  • tried to open 1 window by itself
    Change: open a window on the reader's action only

Points to decide (5)

What looks like a heading is one

Text that looks like a heading and is not marked as one titles the page for the eye only. RGAA 9.1 · WCAG 1.3.1 · thresholds (tool) · light work

  • “Set large and not marked” is set at 28 px, the running text at 16 px, and it is not a heading
    Change: if it titles what follows, mark it h1 to h6
    body > main:nth-of-type(1) > div.big:nth-of-type(1)
  • “Set bold before plain text” is set bolder than the text that follows, and it is not a heading
    Change: if it titles what follows, mark it h1 to h6
    body > main:nth-of-type(1) > div.bold:nth-of-type(2)

What looks like a list is one

A marked list announces how many items it holds and lets a reader move from one to the next. RGAA 9.3 · WCAG 1.3.1 · light work

  • 3 lines in a row begin like list items, from “• First of three”
    Change: mark them as a list: <ul> or <ol>, with <li>
    body > main:nth-of-type(1) > p:nth-of-type(5)

The page does not reload or leave on a timer

A page that reloads or leaves on a timer takes the reader's place, and their time, away from them. RGAA 13.1 · WCAG 2.2.1 · light work

  • reloads itself every 600 s
    Change: let the reader stop or delay it

Videos carry captions

Captions carry speech and sound to those who do not hear them, and to anyone reading with the sound off. RGAA 4.3 · WCAG 1.2.2 · heavy work

  • video without a caption track
    Change: if it carries speech, add <track kind="captions">
    body > main:nth-of-type(1) > video:nth-of-type(1)

The reading

A measure, not a verdict. The page gives its reader 21 things to take in. Its markup says which of them can be taken independently: a heading opens a section, a list holds items, a table's headers free its cells. What it does not declare independent has to be taken in order.

Shown and not said
2: what the eye is given and assistive technology is not.
Said and not shown
0: what is given to assistive technology alone, such as a link past the menu.
Depth
18: the longest run the markup obliges. Without structure it would be 21.
One at a time, by ear
19 steps: each thing that is said, one after the other.
Up to 4 at a time
18 steps as the page stands; 18 with nothing failing. A page of this size and depth takes between 18 and 18.
What the page says
254 bytes of text, by an estimate; 15 bytes travel for each.

The reader who holds up to 4 is a convention, as a reference device is in a test of speed: it takes what comes first on the page, and fewer things at once where the page fails them.

What was not looked at

These rules touch 36 of the 106 criteria of the RGAA 4.1.2, 14 of the 78 of the RGESN 2024, and 4 paragraphs of article 50 of the AI Act. To touch a criterion is not to settle it: most ask for a person's judgement, or for a declaration of the owner's, beyond what a rule can see.

One page was observed at one moment and at two widths. What appears only after an action (a menu opened, a form sent, a dialogue), what stands behind a login, and what lies inside frames from other sites were not observed.
