Automated observation of one rendered page, by rule. It is not a conformity audit: a rule decides what a rule can, and puts the rest to a person.

Method

A browser opened each page as a reader's would, at 1280 px and again at 320 px, and recorded what it showed: the names assistive technology is given, the colours as rendered, the bytes that travelled. 44 criteria then read that record. Each is a rule in plain code; no trained model takes part, so the same record gives the same report.

The reading

A measure, not a verdict. The page gives its reader 17 things to take in. Its markup says which of them can be taken independently: a heading opens a section, a list holds items, a table's headers free its cells. What it does not declare independent has to be taken in order.

Shown and not said
5: what the eye is given and assistive technology is not.
Said and not shown
0: what is given to assistive technology alone, such as a link past the menu.
Depth
16: the longest run the markup obliges. Without structure it would be 17.
One at a time, by ear
12 steps: each thing that is said, one after the other.
Up to 4 at a time
17 steps as the page stands; 16 with nothing failing. A page of this size and depth takes between 16 and 16.
What the page says
126 bytes of text, by an estimate; 4319 bytes travel for each.

The order of mending. Each line mends one criterion, after those above it, and says how many steps then remain. The gains add up, down to the page with nothing failing.

  1. “The page states its language”: 17 steps
  2. “Zoom is not blocked”: 17 steps
  3. “No horizontal scrolling at 320 px”: 16 steps

The reader who holds up to 4 is a convention, as a reference device is in a test of speed: it takes what comes first on the page, and fewer things at once where the page fails them.

What was not looked at

These rules touch 36 of the 106 criteria of the RGAA 4.1.2, 14 of the 78 of the RGESN 2024, and 4 paragraphs of article 50 of the AI Act. To touch a criterion is not to settle it: most ask for a person's judgement, or for a declaration of the owner's, beyond what a rule can see.

One page was observed at one moment and at two widths. What appears only after an action (a menu opened, a form sent, a dialogue), what stands behind a login, and what lies inside frames from other sites were not observed.
