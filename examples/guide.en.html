The criteria

69 rules, each in plain code. For each: what it checks, what that is for, and the text it comes from. They touch 101 of the 106 criteria of the RGAA 4.1.2, 14 of the 78 of the RGESN 2024, and 4 paragraphs of article 50 of the AI Act.

The page has a title

Shown in tabs, bookmarks, search results, and first by screen readers.

RGAA 8.5 · WCAG 2.4.2

The page states its language

Sets screen reader voice, hyphenation and translation.

RGAA 8.3 · WCAG 3.1.1

The page declares its document type

Keeps the browser in standards mode.

RGAA 8.1

Zoom is not blocked

Enlarging the page is the only adjustment some readers have.

RGAA 10.4 · WCAG 1.4.4

No horizontal scrolling at 320 px

320 px is a small phone, or a page zoomed to 400%. Content scrolls one way only.

RGAA 10.11 · WCAG 1.4.10 · RGESN 2.5

The main content is marked

Lets a reader skip what every page repeats.

RGAA 9.2 · 12.6 · WCAG 2.4.1

A link leads to the main content

Spares keyboard users the repeated links on every page.

RGAA 12.7 · WCAG 2.4.1

Heading levels follow each other

Headings are the page's table of contents for assistive technology.

RGAA 9.1 · WCAG 1.3.1

What looks like a heading is one

A heading styled but not marked is invisible to assistive technology.

RGAA 9.1 · WCAG 1.3.1 · thresholds (tool)

What looks like a list is one

A marked list states its length and allows moving item to item.

RGAA 9.3 · WCAG 1.3.1

No element is used as a gap

Empty elements are still announced. Spacing belongs in CSS.

RGAA 8.9 · WCAG 1.3.1

Appearance is left to the style sheet

Styling in markup overrides the reader's own settings.

RGAA 10.1 · WCAG 1.3.1

Images carry a text alternative

What an image says to screen readers, search engines and slow connections.

RGAA 1.1–1.3 · WCAG 1.1.1

Text contrasts with its background

Legibility in sunlight, on worn screens, and for low or color-deficient vision.

RGAA 3.2 · WCAG 1.4.3

Links have a name

Screen readers list links out of context. The name is all that remains.

RGAA 6.1–6.2 · WCAG 2.4.4

Links to different places differ in name

Identical names for different destinations cannot be told apart.

RGAA 6.1 · WCAG 2.4.4

Buttons have a name

An unnamed button is announced as “button”, nothing more.

RGAA 11.9 · WCAG 4.1.2

The words shown are in the name

Voice commands use the visible words. They must be in the coded name.

RGAA 6.1 · 11.2 · 11.9 · WCAG 2.5.3

Form fields have a name

The visible name states what the field expects and enlarges its target.

RGAA 11.1 · WCAG 1.3.1

Groups of fields have a legend

The legend names the question a set of choices answers.

RGAA 11.6 · WCAG 1.3.1

Choices of one question are grouped

Choices to one question are announced together.

RGAA 11.5 · WCAG 1.3.1

Fields for personal data state their purpose

Declared purposes let browsers fill fields: less typing, fewer errors.

RGAA 11.13 · WCAG 1.3.5

Data tables declare their headers

Headers announce each cell with its row and column.

RGAA 5.3 · 5.6–5.8 · WCAG 1.3.1

Frames have a title

The title says what a frame contains before it is entered.

RGAA 2.1 · WCAG 4.1.2

No sound starts by itself

Sound that starts alone drowns out the screen reader.

RGAA 4.10 · WCAG 1.4.2

Videos carry captions

Speech and sound for those who cannot hear, or have sound off.

RGAA 4.3 · WCAG 1.2.2

Element ids are unique

Field names, descriptions and anchors point by id. Duplicates point wrong.

RGAA 8.2

The keyboard follows the order of the page

A positive tabindex pulls an element out of reading order.

RGAA 12.8 · WCAG 2.4.3

Nothing hidden takes the focus

Focus on hidden content lands on something never announced.

RGAA 10.8 · WCAG 4.1.2

The focus can be seen

The focus mark is a keyboard user's only position indicator.

RGAA 10.7 · WCAG 2.4.7

The keyboard can leave what it enters

Without a mouse, a trap makes the rest of the page unreachable.

RGAA 12.9 · WCAG 2.1.2

Language codes are valid and right

A wrong code reads the text in the wrong voice.

RGAA 8.4 · 8.7 · 8.8 · WCAG 3.1.1 · 3.1.2

Right-to-left text declares its direction

Without a declared direction, punctuation and numbers land on the wrong side.

RGAA 8.10 · WCAG 1.3.2

Text survives wider spacing

Readers with dyslexia or low vision widen spacing. Fixed boxes then cut the text.

RGAA 10.12 · WCAG 1.4.12

What moves can be stopped

Endless motion pulls attention away and cannot be read past.

RGAA 13.8 · WCAG 2.2.2

What the pointer clicks, the keyboard reaches

An action reachable only by pointer is closed to keyboards and switches.

RGAA 7.3 · WCAG 2.1.1

Words stay without the style sheet

Words set in CSS vanish without styles and are not always read aloud.

RGAA 10.2 · WCAG 1.3.1

A text colour comes with a background

A reader's own background under a set text colour can make it unreadable.

RGAA 10.5

Links stand out from the text around them

A link that looks like its sentence is found by chance.

RGAA 10.6 · WCAG 1.4.1

Emoji have a text alternative

Screen readers announce emoji by their official name, which may not be the meaning.

RGAA 13.5 · 13.6

A CAPTCHA has another way through

A visual test with no alternative locks out anyone who cannot see it.

RGAA 1.4 · 1.5 · WCAG 1.1.1

Office documents are accessible

A PDF without structure is a picture of text.

RGAA 13.3 · 13.4

No window opens by itself

Unrequested windows move the reader without notice.

RGAA 13.2 · WCAG 3.2.1

The page does not reload or leave on a timer

Timed reloads and redirects take control from the reader.

RGAA 13.1 · WCAG 2.2.1

Conversational AI is disclosed

The AI Act requires telling people when they talk to a machine.

AI Act art. 50(1)

Images made by AI say so

AI-made or altered images must be disclosed. The file often states its origin.

AI Act art. 50(2) · 50(4)

Nothing is asked of the device on arrival

Permission requests before any action are answered blind.

RGESN 4.13 · 6.6 · AI Act art. 50(3)

Third parties wait for consent

Each third party opens a connection to another company before consent.

RGESN 4.4

Nothing plays by itself

Autoplay downloads and decodes media no one asked for.

RGESN 4.1

The page has an end

Infinite scroll has no fixed weight and no reachable footer.

RGESN 4.2 · threshold one screen, twice (tool)

Fonts are few and light

Each font file delays text. Reference limit: two families, four variants, 400 kB.

RGESN 4.8

Text travels compressed

Text compresses to a third or less. Uncompressed, the rest is waste.

RGESN 6.3 · threshold 1 kB (tool)

What does not change is kept by the browser

Cached files are not fetched again on the next page or visit.

RGESN 6.2

The page travels by current protocols

HTTP/2 and 3 share one connection. TLS 1.3 connects faster. IPv6 avoids address translation.

RGESN 3.3 · 6.7

Images far down the page wait to be reached

Images far below the fold cost every reader, are seen by few.

RGESN 6.5 · threshold two screens (tool)

Images are not larger than shown

Undisplayed pixels still travel, decode and use memory and battery.

RGESN 6.4 · threshold 2× (tool)

Heavy images use a modern format

AVIF and WebP carry the same image in fewer bytes.
